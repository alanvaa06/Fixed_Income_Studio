Expectations-hypothesis tests

Campbell-Shiller: slope = 1 under the EH (negative means premia move a lot). Fama-Bliss: slope = 0 under the EH (near 1 means forwards forecast returns, not rates). Newey-West t-stats.

How the estimate is built

  1. Factors. Principal components of the monthly zero-coupon panel (1 to 120 months) become the pricing factors; a VAR(1) gives their dynamics.
  2. Excess returns. One-month holding returns of every maturity are regressed on the factor innovations and lagged factors. The loadings on innovations measure exposure to risk; the intercepts and lagged-factor coefficients identify the prices of risk λ0 and λ1.
  3. Recursions. Bond prices follow the affine recursions with the prices of risk (fitted yields) and with them set to zero (risk-neutral yields). Term premium = fitted − risk-neutral. The expectations line is the average expected short rate; the small residual is convexity.
  4. Sanity checks. The Diebold-Li split does the same accounting with the Nelson-Siegel factors (short rate = Level + Slope) and AR/VAR dynamics. The regressions test whether expectations alone explain the curve.
