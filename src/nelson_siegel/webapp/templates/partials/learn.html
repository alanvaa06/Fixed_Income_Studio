Learn the Models

A short tour of the math, the factors, the short-rate models, term premia and how to read the signals.

Nelson-Siegel & Svensson

The Nelson-Siegel form expresses the yield y at maturity t (years) with a decay parameter τ:

y(t) = β0 + β1·[(1 - e-t/τ) / (t/τ)] + β2·{[(1 - e-t/τ) / (t/τ)] - e-t/τ}
  • β0 Level: long-run yield, parallel shifts.
  • β1 Slope: short minus long (y(0) = β0 + β1); negative when upward-sloping.
  • β2 Curvature: the mid-curve hump, peaking near 1.8·τ years.
  • Svensson adds β3·f2(t,τ2), a second hump for the long end; it needs six or more quotes.

The fitter profiles the sum of squared errors over τ on a grid with closed-form betas, then refines locally. Deterministic, no initial guess, restricted to the identifiable τ range.

Short-rate models

Vasicek: dr = κ(θ − r)dt + σ dW
CIR: dr = κ(θ − r)dt + σ√r dW

One state variable, the instantaneous rate r, drives the whole curve. Bond prices are P(t) = exp(A(t) − B(t)·r) in closed form, so yields, forwards and discount factors follow immediately.

  • Time-series estimate (P): regress the proxy on its lag (Vasicek) or the scaled changes (CIR), or maximise the exact transition likelihood.
  • Calibration (Q): fit the same parameters to today's curve. Only the risk-neutral drift is identified; σ comes from the time series.
  • Feller condition 2κθ ≥ σ² keeps CIR rates strictly positive.
  • Half-life ln 2 / κ: years for a shock to halve.

Term premium

yt(n) = (1/n) Σ Et[rt+i] + TPt(n) + convexity

A long yield is what the market expects short rates to average over the bond's life, plus a premium for duration risk. The premium is not observable, so it is estimated with a no-arbitrage model:

  • ACM (2013): principal-component factors, a VAR, excess-return regressions, prices of risk, affine recursions. Term premium = fitted yield − yield with the prices of risk switched off.
  • Diebold-Li split: project Level + Slope with the AR/VAR dynamics and average. Fast and intuitive; ignores no-arbitrage.
  • Campbell-Shiller: regress future yield changes on the slope; a slope of one means expectations explain everything, the usual negative slope means premia move.
  • Fama-Bliss: regress excess returns on forward spreads; a slope near one says forwards forecast returns, not rates.

Curve analytics glossary

  • Carry: yield earned over funding (the horizon rate). Roll-down: price gain from sliding down an unchanged curve. Total beats zero only if the curve realises less than the forwards.
  • Forwards such as 5y5y: the rate the curve implies for a 5-year loan starting in 5 years, (10·y(10) − 5·y(5)) / 5.
  • 2s10s, 5s30s: slope in bps. 2s5s10s butterfly: 2·y(5) − y(2) − y(10), belly rich when negative.
  • Rich / cheap: residual versus the fitted curve. Positive = higher yield than the model, i.e. cheap.
  • Duration, convexity, DV01: price sensitivity to yield. Key-rate durations split it by the part of the curve that moves.
  • PCA: the first three components of daily yield changes are the empirical level, slope and curvature, usually 95%+ of the variance.

Data sources

  • FRED API (key): daily constant-maturity Treasury and TIPS yields, effective fed funds.
  • U.S. Treasury (no key): the daily par yield curve and real yield curve XML feeds.
