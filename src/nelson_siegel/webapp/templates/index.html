Learn the Nelson-Siegel Model

A short tour of the math, the factors, and how to read the curve.

The equation

The Nelson-Siegel functional form expresses the yield y at maturity t (years) with a decay parameter τ:

y(t) = β0 + β1·[(1 - e-t/τ) / (t/τ)] + β2·{[(1 - e-t/τ) / (t/τ)] - e-t/τ}

Three economic factors plus a decay parameter capture nearly any plausible curve shape with only four numbers. The Svensson extension adds a second hump, β3·f2(t,τ2), for curves with two bulges; it needs at least six quotes.

Reading the factors

  • β0 · Level - long-run yield. Parallel shifts of the entire curve.
  • β1 · Slope - short minus long (y(0) = β0 + β1). Negative when the curve slopes upward, positive when inverted.
  • β2 · Curvature - mid-curve hump. Positive = belly above the line through the ends.
  • τ · Decay - where the hump sits: curvature peaks near 1.8·τ years. The fitter only searches τ values whose hump falls inside your quoted maturities; a "τ at search bound" badge means the data barely pins it down.

How the fit works

  1. For each candidate τ on a fine grid, the betas are solved exactly by least squares (the model is linear once τ is fixed).
  2. The best candidates are refined with a bounded search; the lowest error wins. No initial guess, fully deterministic.
  3. RMSE (in basis points) and R² summarise the fit. The dotted forward curve is d[t·y(t)]/dt, the market's implied path of short rates.
  4. Historical factors reuse one τ per bond type, estimated on the whole sample (the Diebold-Li convention), so the factor series are comparable across dates.

Curve shapes & signals

Normal

Upward-sloping. Growth-positive regime.

Inverted

Long < short. Classic recession signal.

Humped

Mid-tenor peak. Mid-cycle uncertainty.

Flat

Tight term premium. Late-cycle.

How to use this app

  1. Open Curve Fitter, paste market quotes (or click Load latest snapshot).
  2. Hit Fit curve. Read off level/slope/curvature/decay and the residuals.
  3. Switch to Parameter Lab and press Use last fit to see how each factor reshapes the curve.
  4. Use Historical Factors for time series and Treasury vs TIPS for breakevens. Quick-range chips set the dates; every chart exports to CSV.
  5. Keyboard: Alt+1…5 switch tabs.
